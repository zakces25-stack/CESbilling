How a book is checked before it goes live

The header row is read here, in the browser, and hashed before the file goes anywhere. If that hash is not registered for the supplier the book is refused and the header is shown for someone to look at: suppliers change their layouts without telling anyone, and a parser that half-works does not fail, it produces a plausible wrong price. Each file is matched to its product by its name, a book whose own end date has passed is not loaded, and a book older than the one already live is never put over it.

Pick the supplier once. Every file in the drop is then matched against that supplier's products, and anything that does not match is listed rather than guessed at.

Where this meter sits

Gas postcode → LDZ and exit zone

Full postcode, always. 348 of 3,100 national outcodes map to more than one exit zone depending on the incode, so an outcode-only answer is wrong for 11% of sites and looks entirely normal on a quote. If the full postcode is not held and the outcode is ambiguous, this returns nothing rather than a guess.

MPAN top line → TCR band and non-energy charges

Distributor id comes from the first two digits of the MPAN core. The LLFC carries the band: for distributors 10–23 it is the trailing digits, for independent networks 24–27 it is the last letter (F, G, H, J, K = no residual, 1, 2, 3, 4). Charges are the 2026/27 charging year and refresh annually.

Pricebook status bar

How old a supplier’s newest live book may be before its bar changes colour. Weekly is CES’s rule (green under 7 days, yellow 7 to 10, red from day 11 or with no book). Daily is E.ON Next’s: green only with a book valid today. Monthly is for suppliers who issue one book a month (green under 31 days, yellow 31 to 34, red from day 35): British Gas, BG Lite, ScottishPower and SEB electricity do, and under the weekly rule they turn red around the 11th while their book is still the current one.

Add a product
For when a supplier launches something new.

How its file is recognised is a bit of the filename that is the same every month. standard_elec_acq works for every release; standard_elec_acq_04092026 works once and then stops. Leave the date and the version number out.

Checked before matters when one product's name sits inside another's. British Gas send both Standard_Gas_Acq_DSC and Standard_Gas_Acq, and the DSC one has to be checked first or every DSC book loads as the plain one at DSC prices.
